Decision options

Recommended option: controlled recovery with staged approval

Freeze and stabilize

Speed: Fast risk reduction
Benefit: Clear ownership and fewer unassessed changes
Trade-off: Could delay new scope
Risk: Customer demand may increase

Add capacity immediately

Speed: Potentially faster delivery
Benefit: More hands on critical work
Trade-off: Cost and onboarding lead time
Risk: May not address root dependencies

Controlled recovery Recommended

Speed: Staged and measurable
Benefit: Balances commitments, capacity and scope
Trade-off: Requires governance and checkpoints
Risk: Recovery can slip without executive ownership

Choose the controlled option because it reduces the risk created by overload and blocked work without assuming that more people or more hours alone will improve delivery.

Weighted score · sensitivity

Scenario calculation

urgency_after = urgency − delay_days × 0.8
feasibility_after = feasibility + capacity_change × 0.6
scenario_score = baseline − original contributions + modified contributions

Values are clamped to 0–100. Capacity ranges from −50% to +50%; delay ranges from 0 to 90 days.

Assumptions & responsible practice

Every number is traceable, and every decision remains human-owned

Evidence basis

  • The portfolio figures 5 programs, 3 customers, 71% on-time, 17 open dependencies, 6 overdue, 14 changes, 2 critical specialists, and 17% vs 22% margin are from the supplied README and baseline data.
  • Individual program values and top-driver calculations are implemented synthetic data and an illustrative formula.
  • The simulator does not use real historical delivery or financial models.

Assumptions labelled

  • Portfolio-level figures are decision context, not observed outcomes in this MVP.
  • A capacity change is modeled as a linear sensitivity adjustment, not a staffing forecast.
  • Delay reduces urgency as an illustrative proxy for prioritization sensitivity.
  • Human leadership validates customer commitments, contract terms, skills, cost, quality and margin.
