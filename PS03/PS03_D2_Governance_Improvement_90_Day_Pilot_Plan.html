The operating plan · days 1–90

Run a two-engagement pilot with clear gates, named owners and measurable safeguards.

Test one light assurance routine across one software-engineering engagement and one IT-service engagement. Establish pilot baselines first; make a scale, revise or stop decision only after evidence and service guardrails are checked.

Plan in one sentence

Reuse current evidence sources, review six health dimensions monthly, inspect Red actions weekly, and require independent closure verification before reporting improvement.

Pilot clock starts 05 Nov 2026 · D30: 05 Dec 2026 · D60: 04 Jan 2027 · D90: 03 Feb 2027

01 · Governance workflow

Move from a source record to a verified action in one visible chain.

One shared review routine replaces parallel status decks; engagement teams keep ownership of source data and corrective work.

Monthly portfolio · weekly Red actions
01 · COLLECTSource evidence

Engagement leads submit period, source, numerator / denominator, owner and date for six dimensions.

02 · VALIDATECheck confidence

Control Reviewer samples evidence; missing or stale data is Unknown and assigned for follow-up.

03 · RATEApply visible rules

Delivery Assurance Lead applies agreed thresholds; records dimension status and reason separately.

04 · DECIDEEscalate by trigger

Monthly review chooses contain, correct, accept with rationale, or request evidence; critical risks escalate immediately.

05 · VERIFYClose with proof

One accountable owner, due date and measure per action; reviewer checks closure evidence and effect.

Review cadence & escalation

  • Before each review: Engineering and Service Managers submit source-linked measures and explain changes.
  • Weekly: owner and Delivery Assurance Lead age Red / overdue actions; customer-impacting or critical service/release events follow the existing incident process immediately.
  • Monthly: sponsor reviews overall status, top risks, Unknown measures, decisions and overdue actions; status can be challenged with evidence.
  • D30 / D60 / D90: steering checkpoint records continue / revise / stop / scale, rationale, owner and next review date.

Rating and closure controls

  • Show six dimensions: delivery predictability; release quality; service stability; customer impact; findings / action health; evidence traceability.
  • Keep rating separate from evidence confidence, source and as-of date. A critical verified trigger cannot be hidden by an average.
  • Every action links to risk or evidence, has one accountable owner, due date, 30/60/90 horizon, expected outcome and success measure.
  • Owner-reported “Completed” is not “Verified”; a Control Reviewer records evidence and effectiveness check.
  • Record accepted exceptions and management overrides with reason, approver and expiry / re-review date.

03 · Pilot scope, benefits and safeguards

Use contrasting examples to test fairness; do not treat them as portfolio facts.

Added illustrative scenario values

Engineering exemplar · ENG-007

Application scenario: milestone predictability 97%; failed / rolled-back releases 25%; high defect leakage. Test that delivery can remain Green while release quality is Red and the overall status reflects the verified critical quality risk.

Pilot focus: source-linked release sample, defect leakage, go/no-go decision and root-cause follow-up.

Service exemplar · SRV-012

Application scenario: SLO compliance 99%; no material breach or penalty; sampled release traceability 64%. Test that service stability stays Green while evidence traceability is Amber and the overall rating explains the control gap.

Pilot focus: evidence capture and sample verification; do not infer poor service performance from missing records.

Evidence boundary: These named engagement IDs, rates and “no material breach or penalty” case details are added illustrative application scenarios, not the contest’s supplied portfolio statistics. All additional denominator examples—11/50 repeat findings, 9/29 overdue actions and 16/25 traceable releases—are illustrative only; preserve the contest’s 22%, 31% and 64% figures as the source facts.

Expected benefits · hypotheses to test

  • Earlier visibility of weak releases and operational instability.
  • More consistent and fair engagement ratings with evidence confidence shown separately.
  • Improved audit readiness through linked, sampled evidence.
  • Reduced duplicated governance preparation effort.

Cost, dependency and delivery risks

  • Cost: protected staff time and possible source integration. No financial budget or license price was supplied; estimate before approval.
  • Dependencies: sponsor, two willing engagement teams, source access, shared definitions and reviewer capacity.
  • Risk: reporting burden or metric gaming. Mitigate by reusing existing evidence, limiting measures to six, showing denominators and auditing samples.
  • Risk: customer or service deterioration. Existing incident, release and customer-escalation controls remain authoritative; pause and escalate on adverse guardrail change.
