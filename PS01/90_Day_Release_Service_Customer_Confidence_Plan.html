Workstreams, owners, and evidence

Role names are proposed; assign actual accountable people before mobilization.

Application delivery & release

Accountable: Release Director

Objective: recover a small, supportable release slice without carrying unresolved blocking defects into production.

  1. Days 0–3: map remaining stories to defects, dependencies, customer value, and rollback path.
  2. Days 4–14: resolve and retest blockers; pause blocked legacy migration and dependent bulk-upload / seasonal dashboard scope.
  3. Days 15–30: release R15 only if scope isolation, regression, rollback, monitoring, and customer acceptance are evidenced.
  4. Days 31–90: sequence remaining scope from observed reliability and capacity.

Production service & reliability

Accountable: Service Operations Lead

Objective: protect live service and prevent recovery work from hollowing out incident response.

  1. Maintain the baseline 72 support hours/week; treat the 60-hour floor as a synthetic planning assumption pending Operations approval.
  2. Assign incident commander, on-call owner, incident-to-defect linkage, and daily triage.
  3. Use change freeze for non-essential changes; allow only approved corrective changes with rollback plans.
  4. Track unresolved Sev1/Sev2, incident recurrence, customer-journey impact, and monitoring coverage.

Quality & release assurance

Accountable: Quality Lead

Objective: make readiness visible and repeatable before customer rollout.

  1. Define critical customer journeys and test evidence IDs against a frozen build.
  2. Require zero open release-blocking defects in the proposed scope; document any authorized exception.
  3. Verify rollback, alert thresholds, support runbook, and staffed on-call before canary.
  4. Monitor staged rollout and halt automatically on agreed customer/service thresholds.

Commercial & customer confidence

Accountable: Account Director + Finance

Objective: communicate credibly while exposing recovery cost and margin effects.

  1. Finance validates revenue, cost-to-complete, service-credit exposure, and recovery spend treatment.
  2. Set a capped pilot envelope and approval path for borrowed capacity.
  3. Send a concise customer update at each checkpoint: facts, scope, evidence, risk, next update.
  4. Convert a candidate window to a commitment only after evidence and authorized approvals are recorded.

Readiness and customer-commitment gates

Missing evidence means “not ready”; a slider or AI recommendation cannot waive a gate.

Before a release candidate enters canary

  • 1No unresolved release-blocking high-severity defect in selected customer journeys.
  • 2Critical regression tests passed against recorded build and test-run IDs.
  • 3Rollback tested; monitoring and staffed on-call confirmed.
  • 4Support allocation remains at or above the approved service floor.
  • 5Canary duration and stop thresholds approved by Operations.

Before a customer date is committed

  • 1All release readiness evidence above is verified and current.
  • 2Customer UAT / scope acceptance recorded for the limited release.
  • 3Account, Engineering, Operations, and Quality approvers are named.
  • 4Forecast range, dependencies, and remaining uncertainty are explained.
  • 5Customer communication is reviewed and authorized by the account owner.
