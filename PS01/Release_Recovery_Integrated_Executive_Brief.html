Problem statement understood

A strategic account develops a customer-facing application and also operates its production support. The release is late, defects recur, and leaders have different explanations. The customer sponsor questions contract value and expects a credible recovery date ahead of a high-volume season. Engineering needs testing time, Operations wants a freeze, and Finance needs margin protection.

Leadership decision: choose what to stabilize, what to release or pause, how to organize joint engineering and operations ownership, and what can responsibly be communicated to the customer.

Why use a decision simulator?

A status report shows what has happened. The twin models what could happen under a change in scope, engineering capacity, QA time, canary duration, or spend. It recalculates schedule, service coverage, quality gates, and margin together.

Decision principle: show the assumption changed, the consequence, and the evidence still missing. Do not allow a slider or AI output to turn a forecast into a promise.

Before a release candidate enters canary

  • No unresolved release-blocking high-severity defect in the proposed scope.
  • Critical regression passes against a frozen build; test/build IDs recorded.
  • Rollback tested; monitoring and named on-call owner confirmed.
  • Support remains at or above the approved service floor.
  • Canary duration and stop thresholds approved by Operations.

Before a customer date is committed

  • Readiness evidence is verified and current.
  • Customer scope acceptance is recorded.
  • Engineering, Operations, Quality, and Account approvers are named.
  • Forecast range, dependencies, and remaining uncertainty are explained.
  • Account owner approves customer wording. Sliders and AI cannot waive this gate.
