Rebuild trust before pursuing growth.

A decision-grade recovery and renewal plan for a strategic digital-product and managed-services account.

Decision: Defer broad transformation or expansion for 90 days. Ring-fence recovery capacity, resolve the highest-impact customer failures, and establish credible evidence of value before advancing commercial growth.

03 · CRITICAL DIAGNOSIS

Service compliance is not customer confidence.

The operating mismatch

Support SLAs are mostly green while the customer experiences intermittent checkout failures, slow feature improvement, unresolved defects, and poor communication.

  • Product reliability: unresolved customer-facing defects are harming adoption and trust.
  • Communication: customers lack predictable, transparent recovery updates.
  • Value credibility: most promised benefits have not been measured.
  • Capacity conflict: expansion would pull engineers from essential recovery work.

Senior delivery position

Do not solve capacity through sustained overtime or premature hiring assumptions. Protect a named recovery team and make trade-offs explicit to both leadership and the customer.

RECOVERY FIRST NO UNQUALIFIED GROWTH

04 · RECOVERY STRATEGY

Sequence recovery, value proof, and commercial readiness.

STEP 01

Stabilize

Classify every defect by customer harm. Contain customer-blocking failures immediately.

STEP 02

Prove value

Agree measures, baselines, and evidence for historical value claims.

STEP 03

Rebuild trust

Deliver weekly customer updates and monthly executive steering governance.

STEP 04

Qualify growth

Only review expansion after recovery gates and customer acceptance are evidenced.

The $7M pipeline is conditional opportunity—not committed forecast—until recovery capacity can be released without putting customer outcomes at risk.

06 · DECISION GATES

What must be true before expansion is discussed.

Product

No customer-blocking defects remain unresolved; all high-priority issues have a validated plan.

Trust

Weekly commitments are met across two review cycles and acknowledged in customer governance.

Value

At least 8 of 12 claims are verified, formally re-baselined, or retired.

Capacity

Recovery capacity can be released without compromising reliability or customer experience.

07 · GOVERNANCE & LEADERSHIP ASK

Make accountability and proof non-negotiable.

Operating cadence

  • Weekly internal recovery review: Delivery, Product, Engineering, Customer Success.
  • Weekly customer update: defects, releases, risks, decisions, and next update date.
  • Monthly executive steering: sponsor, account lead, customer CIO and key representatives.
  • Immediate escalation: new blocker, missed recovery milestone, capacity conflict, or confidence deterioration.

Leadership decisions this week

  • Approve protected 90-day recovery capacity.
  • Confirm executive sponsor and accountable owners.
  • Approve weekly and monthly customer governance.
  • Remove unverified claims from commercial messaging.
  • Reclassify growth pipeline as conditional opportunity.

How trust is built

By making customer-impacting work visible, meeting the next commitment reliably, and using independently understandable evidence—not by presenting a more optimistic status narrative.

Executive approach

Protect recovery capacity. Lead with product reliability and transparent communication. Validate value with the customer. Use the 90-day review as the evidence-based decision point for renewal confidence and only then for controlled growth.

The desired end-state is not simply fewer defects. It is a customer who can see progress, verify outcomes, and trust the team to keep its next commitment.

Facts vs. proposed targets

Supplied facts: USD 24M annual revenue; nine-month renewal horizon; advocacy 48/100 versus 70 target; 11 unresolved user-facing defects; 4 of 12 claims evidenced; USD 7M uncertain pipeline.

Proposed targets: all defects classified by Day 30, critical customer-blocking defects contained by Day 30, evidence disposition for all claims by Day 30, and at least 8 of 12 claims resolved by Day 90. These require customer agreement.

Delivery constraints and dependencies

Recovery depends on protected specialist capacity, engineering validation before release, customer availability to agree measures, and executive action when recovery milestones conflict with growth work.
