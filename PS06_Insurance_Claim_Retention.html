Executive summary · one-page decision

AI-assisted claim recovery should be approved as a supervised retention service, not an autonomous decision system

Recommendation: approve a controlled 90-day pilot that consolidates synthetic policy, claim, complaint and interaction records, explains the rules-based risk score, drafts authorized outreach, and requires licensed or supervisory review before customer-facing action.

0–100Explainable baseline score. High ≥60; Medium 35–59; Low ≤34. Source: frontend rules engine.
100% gatePricing and coverage actions require human approval. Explicit product requirement; not a measured business outcome.
5 tabsShorter, role-based review flow. Risk, recommendation, support, human review and audit trail.

Decision requested

Approve: Product Owner and Compliance Manager sign off on pilot scope and communication guardrails by 12 November 2026.

Approve: Renewal Underwriter and Customer Advocacy owners validate rules and escalation criteria by 20 November 2026.

Authorize: a 90-day controlled pilot with synthetic/approved records only.

Top risk reduced

Risk: an automated recommendation could change coverage, price, or claim expectations without authority.

Control: rules-grounded output, retrieved guideline citations, communication compliance checks, human review and immutable-style audit events before action.

Why this can be acted on now

The repository already provides a working Next.js workspace, a transparent rules engine, a FastAPI analysis service, SQLite review/audit persistence, and a service-recovery action model. The immediate gap is production governance—not the basic experience.

Evidence basis: repository code reviewed at this date. All customer data in the supplied application is synthetic/demo data. No business uplift is claimed because no outcome data was supplied.

Problem understanding

Retention teams lack a consolidated, traceable view before a renewal or service deadline 03

Business problem

Specialists must reconcile policy records, claims, complaints, interactions and guidelines across systems. This creates slower response, inconsistent follow-up and missed escalation risk.

Root causes

  • Signals are stored across different sources.
  • Risk weights are not consistently visible.
  • Recommended action, approval and outreach are not one review workflow.
  • Audit context can be separated from the decision.

Recommendation and design

Recommended flow keeps every customer-facing commitment under licensed human control 05

AI may assist with

  • Premium-change explanation and eligible option review.
  • Claim delay, denial and missing-update summaries.
  • Complaint deadline, repeat-contact and escalation monitoring.
  • Negative-service follow-up and supervisor callback routing.
  • Coverage-fit review invitation and portal onboarding support.

AI must not independently do

  • Promise claim reopening, payment or guaranteed savings.
  • Set a price or change coverage without authority.
  • Send outreach after customer opt-out or without consent.
  • Override underwriting, claims or regulatory requirements.
  • Record a decision without actor, rationale and timestamp.
